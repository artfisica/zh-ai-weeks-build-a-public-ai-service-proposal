# A six-week pilot: one life change, two communes

**Proposal for discussion with Public AI Switzerland, 8 October 2026.** This is an invitation, not an existing partnership or a municipal service.

## The problem we can test

A family moving from Nyon to Saint-Cergue may need to coordinate the two communes, the Canton of Vaud and federal rules. The same person may wonder what changes if they move to Gland instead. Existing pages answer parts of the problem. This prototype makes the path, the open questions and the effect of changing a fact visible in one persistent map.

The current demo is generated by Apertus. It controls the URL catalogue but does **not** prove that the model chose the right page for a step. No municipality has reviewed the content. The pilot is about making that boundary operational.

One promising real fork to verify with the offices: on 6 October, Vaud's eDéménagement page listed Nyon and St-Cergue among participating communes, while Gland did not appear in that list. The page also distinguishes a departure announced online from an arrival that may need to be announced in person. This is the kind of local difference the map should reveal, with the page date and competent office visible; absence from a list is not itself an official decision about an individual's eligibility.

## Narrow pilot scope

One journey: moving between Nyon and Saint-Cergue, with Gland as a comparison. Two variations: owner versus tenant, and an EU/EFTA B permit versus a case that requires a different check. We will keep school, transport and benefits as linked questions only when a source and a responsible office are identified. We will not calculate eligibility, tax or permit decisions.

## What six weeks produce

| Time | Concrete output | Who must validate it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Weeks 1–2 | A versioned source pack with exact page URLs, one narrow claim per page, scope, last checked date, reviewer and contact office. An explicit “unreviewed” state remains possible. | A municipal or cantonal content owner for claims in their remit |
| Weeks 2–3 | A small deterministic layer that uses reviewed conditions to select steps and questions. Apertus interprets the person's words, suggests wording and explains changes; it does not decide the rule. | Project team and domain reviewer |
| Weeks 4–5 | Five observed usability sessions with residents, including at least one French and one English case, and mobile use. Record where people cannot tell what changed or where a source came from. | Participants consent to testing; results reported without their personal stories |
| Week 6 | A public demonstration, a short evaluation report, and a decision: expand the pack, revise the design, or stop. | Public AI and participating content owners |

## Pass or fail questions

- Can a resident trace every consequential step to an exact source and the competent office, or see an explicit gap?
- When the arrival commune or permit answer changes, are the affected steps and unchanged steps understandable without reading a second essay?
- Do repeated inputs with the same reviewed facts yield the same required steps? Does the model's explanation remain visibly distinct from the reviewed rule?
- Can one small commune maintain its pack without touching the shared application code?
- Can the service run with a dependable inference endpoint, a published privacy account, bounded cost, and no presenter-operated Codespace?

## The ask for Public AI

Would Public AI help convene a six-week discovery pilot? The practical help would be a technical counterpart for inference and hosting, an introduction to one municipal content owner and one Vaud subject specialist, and a weekly 30-minute review. I will maintain the open prototype, implement the pack and rule layer, and run the resident tests. We should agree the scope, ownership and resources together before contacting a commune as a partner.

The decision at week six is whether there is a credible path to a reviewed, maintainable service for other communes. The current demo is evidence that the interaction is possible, not evidence that the civic content is already safe to deploy.
